# The canonical article

One article, adapted for every surface. Written for the person who just lost
a pet and wants something with their name on it — not for a search engine.

## Long form (blog / Substack / Hashnode / WordPress)

**Title:** I built a free memorial shadow box maker, because nobody should have
to pay to see their dog's name on a wall

**Body:**

On the 14th of October my neighbour's dog died. Eighteen years old. The
grief part is not the interesting part. The interesting part is what happens
after: you want one object in the house that says she was here, and every
version of that object you can find is either ugly or costs money you do not
want to spend on a piece of cardstock.

So I built one. It is free, it runs in the browser, and it puts a name on a
shadow box in about ten seconds.

**Type the name. That is the whole thing.**

Open the maker, type her name, and the design changes as you type. Nine
motifs — paw print, rainbow bridge, angel wings, a candle, a heart, a leaf,
the sea, a star, a cross — and nine colourways, all midnight blue and pearl
and gold, because that is the only palette I could find that does not look
like a birthday card.

**The name never leaves your browser.** It is written into the fragment of the
address, the part after the `#`, and a browser never sends that to a website
and a search engine never indexes it. So the name of the one you lost is not
on my server, not in my logs, and will not turn up in a search for your name
one day. I built it that way on purpose, and it is the part I would push back
on if I were reviewing someone else's version of this.

**Then the part that is worth paying for.** The free version is a picture.
The paid version is the thing you can actually make: seven separate SVG
layers, sized for Cricut and Silhouette and laser cutters, with the name
plate left as editable text. You type the name into the front layer in
Cricut Design Space, right up until you cut it. Nothing else lets you change
the spelling of a name after you have committed to it, and if you have ever
spelled a pet's name wrong on a keepsake you know that is not a small thing.

Seven layers is the whole trick of a shadow box. The voids in the upper
layers reveal the layers beneath, and that is where the depth comes from.
Cardstock between 250 and 350 gsm, foam tape on the back of each layer, and
about an hour with a glass of wine.

**Try it before you buy anything:** the free maker is at the link below, and
it needs no account, no email, no signup. If it is not for you, you have lost
nothing but a minute.

**If you want the cut files:** the bundle is at the storefront link below.
Pay with USDT, no account needed, and the files land in your inbox
automatically. If you would rather buy it with a card, mail me and I will
sort it out.

**One last thing.** If you are stuck on what to write on the plate — and most
people are, for weeks — the honest answer is: write the name. That is not a
cop-out. For most people it is the truest version. If you want a second line,
make it the years, in smaller type, in a lighter colour. And do not write a
sentence, because a sentence turns a keepsake into something people feel
obliged to comment on, and a shadow box is not a conversation. It is looked
at quietly, on a wall, years later.
